# Project brief

Owner-approved direction: 2026-11-09. Features remain planned; see [MILESTONES](MILESTONES.md).

## Problem, aim, and users

Traditional storytellers may reach only people who share their language. AI transcription/translation can widen audiences but lose idioms, tone, and cultural intent. No supplied survey establishes how many people are affected.

Anansi aims to carry isiXhosa stories to English-speaking listeners while preserving original audio and making human correction visible. AI drafts; people judge fidelity. One account can tell, listen, and verify; verification requires both languages self-declared on the profile.

## Objectives

1. Record/upload at most three minutes of isiXhosa audio and correct a segmented transcript.
2. Publish a linked English translation without replacing the original.
3. Let bilingual people approve/reject, rate confidence, correct segments, and explain intent.
4. Let listeners read/hear the original beside the preferred translation with highlighting.
5. Demonstrate simple discovery and a complete journey with limited real-user evidence.

## Capstone alignment and success

Primary: **Voices & Languages** - underserved-language translation, original voice, human checking of nuance. Supporting: **Discovery & Audience** - following, reposts, simple discovery. Consent, attribution, and traceability support authorship care without claiming legal ownership protection.

The capstone requires a working, useful, viable proof of concept built using AI software development tools, not production software. A proposal alone is insufficient. Success is the tested [core journey](PRODUCT_SCOPE.md), bilingual feedback, and two uninterrupted five-minute rehearsals. One tester cannot prove population-level accuracy.
